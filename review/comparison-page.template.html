Assumptions, calculations and sources

Electrical scenario

20 mΩ is an illustrative combined resistance for the cell, leads and contacts upstream of the converter. It is not a measured specification for any of these cells. The same selected resistance applies to every size.

  • Representative open-circuit voltage: 3.8 V.
  • Loaded-voltage cutoff: 3.2 V.
  • Boost efficiency: 90%. Phone charging efficiency: 85%.
  • Available stored energy before resistance losses: 80% of nameplate, with 65% and 90% alternatives. These are assumptions for charge ceiling, cutoff reserve and aging.
  • Phone battery: __PHONE_WH__ Wh, the eSIM iPhone 18 Pro basis.

The model holds voltage and resistance constant. It checks one operating point and does not predict a full discharge curve, temperature, taper or thermal shutdown.

Evaluation targets are 10, 15 and 25 W for the 300, 530 and 850. All output figures are calculations. Complete phone-output hardware and measurements are still required.

Energy and heat

Converter input P = USB watts ÷ 0.90
Current I = 2P ÷ (V + √(V² − 4RP))
Loaded voltage = V − IR
Cell + wiring heat = I²R
Runtime = stored Wh ÷ (P + I²R)
Phone Wh = (USB watts × 0.85 − phone-use watts) × runtime
Added points = phone Wh ÷ __PHONE_WH__ Wh × 100

A negative square-root term or loaded voltage at/below cutoff gives no runtime or gain estimate. Heat figures are watts lost, not temperatures. Actual cell resistance varies with charge, age and temperature.

Does higher power change the size?

Capacity in mAh does not set the discharge limit. The same cell can serve two power levels only if its current, voltage sag and temperature permit both. A different cell may have different dimensions even at the same capacity.

The table keeps the listed cells fixed. A higher output can need a different converter, inductor, protection circuit, contacts and heat path. These may require a larger case. The drawings do not certify a 40 or 60 W build.

At 60 W and 90% converter efficiency, a 3.2 V cell must supply 20.83 A. For 300 mAh that is 69.4C, above the manufacturer's 60C label. The 55 and 60 W rows therefore omit gain and time for that cell. Even below a label, allowable current requires discharge and temperature measurements. Cell specification.

The existing TPS61288 has a 15 A typical peak switch-current limit. A 60 W design needs 20.83 A average at 3.2 V before inductor ripple, so it needs a different power stage. Converter datasheet.
