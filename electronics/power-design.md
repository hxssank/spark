# Spark power design, revision A

Build and measure the 5 V / 9 V boost stage before connecting a cell or a phone. The supplied 20 × 38 mm PCB is a routed converter prototype. It has no USB port, PD controller, charger, battery protection or temperature cutoff. The product case reserves room for a later complete power board; fitting this converter into that space does not prove that all remaining circuits fit.

The 25 W design target requires 9 V at 2.78 A; it is not a demonstrated rating. Output current depends on the attached phone's negotiated contract. A boost converter alone cannot negotiate USB-C charging.

## Circuit and assembly

The PCB uses TPS61288LRQQR and a Coilcraft XAL1060-222MEC 2.2 µH inductor. U1 uses TI's RQQ0011A land drawing, including the irregular pads. The inductor uses Coilcraft's land pattern, rotated 90 degrees. Match its marked short winding terminal to SW. TI's converter datasheet and evaluation board provide the circuit basis. This layout and its 5 V / 9 V compensation are new and untested. [TI datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps61288.pdf), [TI evaluation board](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/SLVUBW6), [Coilcraft drawing](https://www.coilcraft.com/getmedia/8909f858-b441-4d60-acff-8b8ca36f9ede/xal1060.pdf)

Use four layers, 1.0 mm board thickness, 2 oz outer copper and 1 oz inner copper. The board file and Gerber job specify 70/35/35/70 µm copper. The proposed dielectric split is 0.14/0.51/0.14 mm; the fabricator must confirm its available stackup and finished thickness. In1 is GND; In2 distributes VIN. The back copper distributes VOUT. Small vias require a fabricator supporting 0.20 mm drills and 0.15 mm trace/clearance. The via-in-pad locations need a fabrication and stencil review before assembly. A passing clearance check does not establish acceptable solder wicking or thermal performance.

The silkscreen labels the supply polarity, output polarity, enable pad and voltage selector. The dot next to U1 marks pin 1. Mount L1's marked short winding lead toward the SW label and U1. The SW trace widens from 0.4 mm at the package escape to 0.8 mm and then 2.0 mm; its 0.4 mm section is 1.55 mm long. The high-current copper still requires temperature and voltage-drop measurements.

R1 = 110 kΩ and R2 = 15 kΩ set 5.000 V nominal. Closing JP1 places R3 = 16.5 kΩ in parallel with R2 and sets 9.000 V nominal. Change JP1 only with input power disconnected. R5 pulls EN low by default. Drive J5 from an external 3.3 V control source referenced to J2; 1.2 V is the specified logic-high boundary, so it provides no margin. Keep the applied enable voltage within the prototype's 1.2–4.2 V range. The PCB has no output disconnect switch, so EN is not a safe isolation control.

J1/J2 accept an isolated, current-limited laboratory supply set between 3.0 and 4.2 V. J3/J4 connect to an electronic load. The circuit has no connector keyed for a battery or phone.

## Calculated operating margin

These numbers are design calculations, not test results.

At 25 W and 90% conversion efficiency, input current is 6.61 A at 4.2 V, 7.72 A at 3.6 V and 9.26 A at 3.0 V. Converter loss is 2.78 W before battery, connector and wiring loss. A 10 mΩ connection adds 0.86 W at 9.26 A. That loss makes small contacts and thin leads material design choices.

For 9 V / 3 A at 3.0 V input, 440 kHz switching and inductance derated 30% to 1.54 µH, estimated ripple is 2.95 A peak-to-peak and peak inductor current is 11.48 A. The nominal 15 A switch-current label is not an allowable continuous battery current. TI lists a 12 A minimum limit at its stated 7.2 V input and 16 V output test condition. At 85% efficiency, the same 9 V / 3 A corner reaches 12.06 A peak. A 3 A setting therefore has no demonstrated low-cell current margin. At the actual 25 W target the estimate is 11.28 A. Verify current limit, efficiency and temperature at 3.0 V before choosing the advertised contract or derating threshold.

Compensation candidates are R4 = 12.4 kΩ, C11 = 8.2 nF and C12 = 10 pF. The calculation uses at least 60 µF effective output capacitance, a 0.6 V reference, 180 µS error-amplifier transconductance and 13.5 A/V power-stage gain. At 3.0 V input, estimated crossover is 1.78 kHz at 9 V / 3 A and 5.76 kHz at 5 V / 3 A. With inductance increased 30% to 2.86 µH, the corresponding right-half-plane zeros are 18.55 and 33.39 kHz. Both crossover estimates lie below one fifth of those zeros.

Six 22 µF output capacitors provide 132 µF nominal. The 60 µF assumption allows a combined 55% reduction for tolerance, voltage bias, temperature and aging; it is an acceptance requirement, not a verified capacitor curve. Use the specified part and confirm effective capacitance under bias before approving the compensation. GRM32ER71E226KE15L is 2.5 ± 0.2 mm thick. The enclosure needs its 2.7 mm maximum plus solder and assembly clearance. [Murata component specification](https://search.murata.co.jp/Ceramy/image/img/A01X/G101/ENG/GRM32ER71E226KE15-04CA.pdf)

The generated [electrical report](boost-prototype/electrical-report.json) calculates these current, loss and compensation cases from `circuit.json`. It separates the 25 W design target from the 3 A stress study, which reaches 27 W at 9 V. The 5 V study stops at 3 A and 15 W. Generation rejects mismatched feedback targets, unsupported changes to the fixed circuit connections and invalid numerical assumptions. The build also rejects a calculated crossover above either screening bound. Passing these calculations does not validate effective capacitance, loop phase margin, low-input current limit or thermal performance.

## Compact board proposal, 2026-10-30

Use separate power stages for the 10/15 W and 25 W versions. The compact placement study targets 17 × 24 mm for Spark 300, 20 × 24 mm for Spark 530 and 20 × 28 mm for Spark 850. These are board reservations, not routed circuits or qualified minimum sizes. The existing 20 × 38 mm bench board and its BOM remain revision A.

For Spark 300 and 530, evaluate BQ25638 with one shared charger/boost inductor. Its 2.07 × 2.36 mm DSBGA body is nominal. The recommended continuous battery-discharge limit is 7 A, with 9 A permitted for at most 50 ms. At 3.0 V and assumed 90% efficiency, 10 W needs 3.70 A and 15 W needs 5.56 A. The 25 W target needs 9.26 A and excludes this path across the intended discharge range. [TI BQ25638 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq25638.pdf)

The low-power inductor candidate is XGL4030-102MEC, 1 µH, with a 4.3 × 4.3 × 3.1 mm maximum body and 7.2 mΩ maximum room-temperature DCR. Calculated DC winding loss at 5.56 A is 0.22 W. The listed currents for 20% and 30% inductance reduction are 7.6 A and 10.3 A. Check inductance tolerance, DC-bias loss and the converter's maximum fault current before selecting it. [Coilcraft family dimensions](https://www.coilcraft.com/en-us/products/power/shielded-inductors/molded-inductor/xgl/), [XGL4030 datasheet](https://www.coilcraft.com/getmedia/032d9c73-4222-482f-b6bc-7808590e27c9/xgl4030.pdf)

For Spark 850, retain TPS61288L and evaluate the 8 × 8 × 3.1 mm XGL7030-222MEC inductor. At 9.26 A its 8.8 mΩ maximum cold DCR gives 0.75 W winding loss before core loss and rising copper resistance. The smaller coil saves space but increases the existing coil's calculated 0.42 W loss. Its 14.5 A rating marks a 30% inductance reduction, so overload remains a design constraint. Onboard charging needs a separate BQ25628 stage, inductor and capacitors, with the boost connected directly to the independently protected pack. [Coilcraft XGL7030 datasheet](https://www.coilcraft.com/getmedia/63e6e233-cff1-40b9-9e5d-f0f490094f44/xgl7030.pdf)

Populate both board faces. Put USB at the short edge, the power loop on the front and low-profile control parts on the back. The display overlaps the electronics bay rather than adding another length allowance. Start the compact study with six copper layers for WLP escape and control routing; four layers remain an option if the completed routing and current paths fit. Preserve a nearby ground plane, short switch-node copper and enough copper for heat spreading. BQ25638's layout instructions explicitly prohibit paralleling its SW connection across layers. Extra layers do not establish thermal capacity. [TI BQ25638 layout instructions](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq25638.pdf), [TI TPS61288 layout instructions](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps61288.pdf)

Use 0201/0402 parts for suitable signal resistors and small bypass capacitors. For the 850 output bank, reserve eight to ten 0805 sites around the power loop for 22 µF, 25 V X5R candidates such as TDK C2012X5R1E226M125AC. Its maximum body is 2.2 × 1.45 × 1.45 mm. This site count is a placement allowance, not an approved population. Require at least 60 µF effective at 9 V after tolerance, bias, temperature and aging, then verify ripple and loop stability. Parallel capacitances add; two equal capacitors in series halve nominal capacitance. Series strings do not provide an assumed area saving. [TDK component specification](https://product.tdk.com/en/search/capacitor/ceramic/mlcc/info?part_no=C2012X5R1E226M125AC)

Consolidate cell gauging and independent protection in MAX17311, 1.7 × 2.5 mm nominal WLP. Do not add MAX17260 or BQ2980 alongside it for the same functions. Keep its external FET pair, Kelvin shunt, cell thermistor and verified configuration. BQ25638's internal converter and charging protections do not replace this independent cell disconnect in the proposal. The compact design must still interrupt overcharge, undervoltage, short circuit and cell overtemperature without working display firmware. A removable cartridge still needs a data contact or a reset of learned capacity after a cell swap. [Analog Devices MAX17311 family datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/max17300-max17313.pdf)

Share STM32G071GBU6N between the display and USB-PD policy. A shared charge/output port uses TCPP03-M20, 4 × 4 × 0.75 mm, plus external VBUS FETs, a current shunt and its bias capacitor. Keep the port isolated until attachment and voltage negotiation permit output. Reserve the logic regulator, OLED supply with its inductor and capacitors, flex connection, wake button and programming pads. These supporting circuits cannot disappear into an IC body box. The 17.5 mm display glass and its unverified flex route remain mechanical constraints. [ST TCPP03 datasheet](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/tcpp03-m20.pdf?icmp=tt39118_gl_lnkon_may2024)

The compact cell-contact reservation assumes a new contact assembly; it does not prove the existing A30 plug and lead bends fit. Retain A30 for the existing sample. A smaller removable interface needs keyed polarity, contact isolation, strain relief and measured temperature and voltage drop near 10 A for the 850. Keep protection with a removable cell cartridge. If protection stays on the main board, the cell is a fixed service assembly and its disconnected leads need separate protection against shorting. The compact reservation does not select or qualify that interface.

## Earlier bench-prototype completion route

This earlier route describes adding source-only USB output to the revision A converter. The compact proposal above instead reserves STM32 UCPD and TCPP03 for a shared charge/output port. Neither complete circuit is implemented.

The earlier circuit is protected 1S cell → TPS61288L → controlled output switch → USB-C socket. An STUSB4710AQTR source controller can select fixed 5 V and 9 V contracts and control the output path. It needs programmed NVM, switched feedback, discharge behavior, output overcurrent protection and CC/ESD protection. Never apply an unnegotiated 9 V to USB-C VBUS. STUSB4710 is source-only and requires at least 4.1 V at VDD. A depleted 1S cell cannot power it directly. This circuit would need to wake the boost into 5 V before attachment detection while holding the output switch off, then manage standby drain and fault shutdown. A PD trigger requests power from a source and cannot perform this source-controller role. This route uses a separate, keyed external charging dock. It cannot support a single bidirectional USB-C port without changing the controller and power paths. [ST controller documentation](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stusb4710.pdf), [ST source evaluation guide](https://www.st.com/resource/en/user_manual/um2208-getting-started-with-the-stevalisc003v1-stusb4710-evaluation-board-usb-pd-controller-with-onboard-dcdc-stmicroelectronics.pdf)

Add independent cell overvoltage, undervoltage, overcurrent and short-circuit protection; cell and PCB temperature sensors; a fuse matched to the verified cell path; and a mechanical power disconnect. Prototype targets are low-cell shutdown at 3.2 V under load, restart above 3.5 V, cell cutoff at 45°C, and case temperature below 42°C. These are proposed project limits, not a manufacturer's qualified operating envelope. The cell supplier's stricter limits take precedence. TPS61288's own undervoltage and thermal shutdown do not substitute for these controls.

Use the GNB 850 mAh cell as a sample candidate. Begin external charging at 0.8 A with a nominal 4.20 V target. [WhoopStor specifies](https://viflydrone.com/products/vifly-whoopstor-6-ports-1s-battery-storage-charger-discharger) ±0.05 V, so that setting can reach 4.25 V and cannot enforce a strict 4.20 V ceiling. A strict ceiling requires a charger or independent termination circuit whose maximum, including tolerance, is 4.20 V. Qualify the exact cell against the actual charger termination. This target is deliberately below the nominal full LiHV voltage; do not credit the full nominal 3.23 Wh in runtime estimates at this setting. Confirm the exact cell's charge ceiling, temperature limits and charged-storage instructions before using a higher voltage. The manufacturer advertises 1C–5C charging, but that does not validate pocket storage or enclosed operation. [GNB product specification](https://www.gaoneng.shop/products/gaoneng-gnb-lihv-1s-3.8v-850mah-60c-a30-cabled-lipo-battery-long-range)

## Battery display and monitoring

Reserve the Ronbo RB060-6464TDDBG01 0.6-inch colour OLED, an SPI-capable low-power MCU and a switched display supply. The compact proposal above uses MAX17311 for both gauge and protection. MAX17260 remains the earlier separate-gauge alternative described here, not an additional compact-board part. The 3 × 3 mm TDFN gauge supports a 1S cell, external current sensing, a pouch-mounted NTC and I2C. Its charge setting must match the actual charger termination voltage, termination current and measured usable capacity. The gauge supports a 2.3 to 4.9 V measurement range; its 3.0 V startup requirement also needs a depleted-cartridge test. Its reported charge, learned capacity, resistance and cycle count are estimates to validate against bench measurements. [Analog Devices MAX17260 datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/max17260.pdf)

Use a 1 mΩ Kelvin-connected sense resistor as the initial candidate. At the calculated 9.26 A input, its loss is 0.086 W. The gauge then has a ±51.2 A current range and 5 mAh capacity resolution, about 0.59% of 850 mAh. A default 10 mΩ gauge breakout clips above 5.12 A and is incompatible with this output target. Its tolerance, pulse rating and layout still need selection. Route both charging and discharging through the same shunt. Keep the gauge paired with the removable protected cartridge and give the host MCU access over I2C. Save learned parameters by cartridge identity. A gauge fixed in the main body cannot identify different raw cells through the current two-wire A30 connector. Until the connector and identity design exists, reset the learned state after a cell swap and display "Health unknown".

The main screen should show remaining charge. Put battery health on a second screen, derived from learned usable capacity divided by a baseline measured with the same charge ceiling, cutoff, temperature and test load. Do not compare capacity measured at a reduced charging voltage directly with the nominal 850 mAh until the supplier provides its capacity-test protocol. Label the result "Capacity health"; it does not establish that a cell is safe. Show "Learning" until a known cartridge has a validated baseline and accepted learning data. Show "Sensor fault" when I2C readings fail or become stale. A coloured arc must never hide these states behind a last-known percentage.

The Ronbo product page could not be retrieved during the 2026-10-29 audit. Its following dimensions remain provisional until the supplier provides the controlled drawing. The provisional [Ronbo panel](https://www.ronboe.com/shop/rb060-6464tddbg01/) has a 15 × 17.5 × 1.2 mm glass envelope, 64 × 64 RGB pixels and a 20-pin, 0.5 mm-pitch SPI FPC. Obtain its controlled electrical drawing, connector orientation, flex bend limits and lifecycle confirmation before routing. The similar [Newhaven 0.6-inch panel](https://newhavendisplay.com/content/specs/NHD-0.6-6464G.pdf) documents a 3.0 V logic supply, 12 V OLED supply and SSD1357 controller. Treat those as planning references only; they do not establish Ronbo pin compatibility or supply requirements. Reserve a separate enabled OLED boost supply and logic rail. The existing 5 V/9 V phone-output converter cannot supply that 12 V reference rail.

Wake the display on a button press or output connection, then turn it off after a short timeout. Use partial framebuffer updates so a small MCU can drive the display. Confirm the selected panel's electrical drawing before fixing its supply voltage, connector, pinout, reset timing or MCU. Measure display-on and standby drain. The boost prototype has no gauge, MCU, display supply or connector; the display is an integration reservation. Independent cell protection and temperature shutdown must still operate if the MCU or display fails.

## Bench acceptance before a battery test

1. Check component orientation, solder joints, rail resistance and regulator feedback with power disconnected. Verify JP1 open and EN low.
2. Start at 3.6 V with a 100 mA input limit and EN low. Check for unintended current. Enable with no output load and confirm the 5 V setpoint. Disconnect before selecting 9 V.
3. Test 0.1 A, 0.5 A, 1 A and then higher output loads at 4.2, 3.6 and 3.0 V input. Raise the supply current limit only as needed for each load. Stop on oscillation, unexplained input current or overheating.
4. Measure output ripple and load steps with a short ground spring. Measure loop gain at both voltage settings and input extremes. Require at least 45° phase margin and 10 dB gain margin. Check startup, shutdown and overload recovery.
5. Log converter, inductor and copper temperatures during the entire intended 25 W run. Confirm the high-current path does not exceed component ratings. Repeat in the enclosure only after open-bench operation passes.
6. Add and test independent cell protection and thermal shutdown before connecting the battery. Add the PD controller and verify actual contracts, hard reset, unplugging and reverse-voltage behavior before connecting a phone.

The current deliverable is the converter design and its geometric checks. None of these hardware acceptance tests has run.

## Rebuild and inspect

From the Spark 2 workspace, with Python and KiCad 10 available:

```sh
python3 electronics/boost-prototype/build_electronics.py
```

Pass `--kicad-cli /path/to/kicad-cli` when KiCad is outside PATH or the local application locations. The builder stages the circuit, PCB and schematic, runs ERC and DRC with schematic parity, and exports the netlist, PDF, Gerbers, drills and SVG preview. Failed tool checks retain the previous exports. It publishes `build-report.json` with input and output hashes only after all checks pass. The package checker rejects stale exports when source or generated files change.

`circuit.json` remains an input. Board generation rereads it on every run and no longer rewrites its values. Regeneration replaces generated CAD; preserve manual layout changes first. The saved project uses 0.15 mm clearance and track rules and a 0.20 mm minimum via drill. The fabrication stackup still needs the board supplier's confirmation.
